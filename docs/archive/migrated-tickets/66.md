## Original issue body

## Problem

The comparison divider — white line and white circle handle — is difficult to see when positioned over bright image regions. The existing black drop shadow helps on dark backgrounds but not on light ones.

## Solution

Add a visible dark outline (stroke) around both the divider line and the circle handle. A thin dark stroke provides contrast against light backgrounds; the white fill provides contrast against dark ones. This is the standard approach used by Lightroom, Affinity Photo, and similar tools.

**Line:** replace the single `shadow` with a layered approach — a semi-transparent black stroke or a double-shadow (one dark, one white glow) to produce a visible border on any background.

**Circle handle:** add `.overlay(Circle().stroke(Color.black.opacity(0.35), lineWidth: 1.5))` outside the existing shadow.

No change to the overall visual design — same white line, same white circle, just more resilient against all backgrounds.

## Paint only. Do not touch the geometry.

The divider's position is pinned by two criteria and this change must leave both untouched:

- **AC90.14**: the divider sits where the pointer is, within the displayed image frame, clamped between 5% and 95% of its width, at any window width, side-panel width, zoom or pan.
- **AC96.4**: the divider addresses the same fraction of width in both pictures whatever their shapes.

UT-90.1 failed once on exactly that geometry and was re-passed only recently. A paint change that alters the line's width, the handle's diameter, or the frame either occupies moves the hit area and breaks both, without touching a line of geometry code.

**RT-66.1** guards it: the divider's frame and the handle's frame are unchanged by the fix.

## These criteria carry no automated floor, deliberately

AC66.1 to AC66.3 are judged by a human, which is the right type: whether a line reads against a photograph is not something a machine decides, and `TESTING.md`'s decision tree sends it to a user test.

The consequence is worth stating rather than discovering later. Beyond RT-66.1's geometry guard there is nothing to stop a later refactor dropping the stroke entirely with every test still green.
