## Original issue body

Child of delivery master #133 (the 2026-08-29 user-test round).

## Problem statement

**Observed by the author, 2026-08-29:**

> *"No route to clear the image and browse for a new image - 3rd time i am raising this to you"*

## Raised three times, and the verification is the reason

**#130 closed on 2026-08-27** having added a **File ▸ Open Image** command on Cmd+O. RT-130.2 and RT-130.3 pass. And I recorded this in that ticket's own code audit:

> *"The open panel itself remains undrivable from XCUITest, so RT-130.2 asserts the command exists and is enabled rather than driving it to a file."*

**So the menu entry was verified and the action was not.** A test that finds a menu item by title proves the item exists; it proves nothing about what happens when a user picks a file.

That is the honest account of why this is being raised a third time: the fix may be real and undiscoverable, or present and broken, and nothing in the suite distinguishes those.

## Two things are being asked for, and only one was built

- **Browse for another picture.** #130's command. Verification gap above.
- **Clear the current picture.** Never built. There is no way to return the application to its empty state --- the drop target with its own chooser --- once a picture is loaded. The author has asked for *"clear the image **and** browse"* each time.

## A concrete candidate, from reading

`SuperscaleApp.swift` declares **two** `CommandGroup(after: .newItem)` blocks in one `.commands` builder --- one added by #130 for Open Image, one pre-existing for Open Recent:

```
155:            CommandGroup(after: .newItem) {   // Open Image…
165:            CommandGroup(after: .newItem) {   // Open Recent
```

Whether SwiftUI honours both at the same placement, or the second replaces the first, decides whether the command is where the author looks. **This is a hypothesis from reading and must be confirmed by looking at the running menu**, not by reasoning about SwiftUI's behaviour --- three defects in this delivery have already been caused by reasoning about a framework instead of measuring it.

## Violated criteria

- Guide **2.2**: *"Accepts PNG, JPEG, TIFF and HEIC via drag and drop, the open panel, or paste."* Unconditionally, not only on an empty canvas.
- **AC89.8** in [docs/ACs.md](../blob/master/docs/ACs.md) carries #130's note and will need correcting if the route is found not to work.
- Nothing covers **clearing** to the empty state. Expect a backfill.

## Debugging and fix procedure

1. **Look at the running application's File menu** with a picture loaded. One observation settles the two-`CommandGroup` question.
2. **Drive the command to a real file** and confirm the picture changes. If XCUITest cannot dismiss the panel, prove it another way --- an `NSOpenPanel` is not the only possible route, and a route that cannot be verified is a route that will be reported a fourth time.
3. **Design the clear.** It returns the canvas to the drop target, which already carries its own chooser --- so clearing may deliver both halves of the request at once, and is testable without a panel.
4. **Settle what happens to the lock chain**, which AC89.8 says an import discards. Clearing is the same question and the author has paid for those results.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC135.1** With a picture on the canvas, the File menu offers both an Open Image command and an Open Recent submenu, and neither displaces the other.
- **AC135.2** A control that clears the current picture is present on the main window whenever a picture is loaded, and absent when the canvas is empty.
- **AC135.3** Once the picture is cleared, the canvas shows the drop target and the drop target's own file chooser is reachable.
- **AC135.4** A picture brought in after a clear becomes the source and appears on the canvas.
- **AC135.5** A cleared session's lock chain is gone from the window; where the session produced a generation, it is still listed under Open Recent. A picture cleared without ever having generated leaves no entry, which is how the recents store has always behaved --- it records generations, not imports.
- **AC135.7** The clear control is unavailable while an upscale or a filter is in flight, so no operation can finish onto an emptied canvas.
- **AC135.6** After a clear, the settings that belong to the picture --- the scale selection, any custom dimensions, the filter result, and any error or notice --- are back at their defaults, while the settings that belong to the user --- the chosen model, face enhancement, button labels, **and whether the comparison curtain is switched on** --- are unchanged. *(Corrected during implementation, DECISION D-6: the comparison was in the picture-specific list and had to come out.)*
- **AC135.7** The clear control is unavailable while an upscale or a filter is in flight, so no operation can finish onto an emptied canvas.
- **AC135.8** A clear takes effect immediately and asks for no confirmation, on the same terms as the import in AC89.8, and an unsaved result goes with it.
- **AC135.9** After a clear the comparison is unavailable, because there is nothing to compare, and it becomes available again on the next picture that derives something --- without the user having to switch it back on.

## DECISION D-5

**Question.** What should the clear control do while an upscale or a filter is running?

**Options.** Cancel the operation and clear; refuse the clear until it finishes; or clear and let the
operation land on an empty canvas.

**Chosen.** **Refuse until it finishes**, expressed as AC135.7.

**Why.** The third option is a defect by construction. Between the first two, cancellation is the
better product and it is not what this ticket is: `UpscaleViewModel` has no cancellation path, a
filter in flight is a paid provider call that cancelling locally would not refund, and building
cancellation inside a defect raised about a missing button is how a three-line fix becomes a
subsystem. Refusal is honest, it is one `.disabled`, and it leaves cancellation available to be
asked for on its own terms.

**Cost if wrong.** A user who wants out of a slow filter waits for it. That is the behaviour today,
so the decision makes nothing worse.

## DECISION D-6

**Found during implementation, and it is a miss by both of my own audits.**

`UpscaleViewModel.releaseUpscaledResult` carries this, put there by #126:

> 🚫 `showComparison = false` is removed by #126. The curtain's visibility is the user's setting and
> the application no longer writes it (guide 2.3).

**AC135.6 as I wrote it listed "the comparison state" among the settings a clear puts back to
default.** Implementing that would have had the application write `showComparison` again — and the
defect it would produce is the one the author has now reported twice: switch the curtain off, clear
a picture, load another, and the curtain is back on. #134 was that report arriving for the second
time, closed four commits ago.

**Question.** Does a clear reset the comparison setting?

**Chosen.** **No.** AC135.6 is corrected to move the comparison out of the picture-specific list and
into the user's, and **AC135.9** is added to say what actually happens: the comparison is
*unavailable* after a clear because the graph is empty, and available again on the next picture that
derives something. Availability is a fact about the graph; visibility is the user's setting. The two
were conflated in the criterion and are now separated.

**Why this is recorded rather than quietly fixed.** The AC audit ran twice and passed, the test
audit ran twice and passed, and neither caught it. What caught it was opening the function the code
had to call. **That is the third time in this delivery that reading the code beat reasoning about
it**, and it is worth the note: an audit of criteria I wrote, by me, against a system I am modelling
from memory, is not a substitute for looking.

**Cost if wrong.** If the author does want the curtain reset by a clear, it is one line and he has
the opposite complaint on record twice, so the burden of evidence is on the reset.
