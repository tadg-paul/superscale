## Original issue body

Child of delivery master #133 (the 2026-08-29 user-test round).

## Problem statement

**Observed by the author, 2026-08-29:**

> *"it is difficult to grab the slider in the curtain comparison widget. I always end up grabbing the image and moving it instead. If we could have the mouse/trackpad scroll move the curtain left and right. I could then toggle off the filter and zoom in, scroll around, re-enable and expect to see the comparison on the zoomed in portion. Though i could still grab the image to move it about, so zooming in to inspect it is still possible either way."*

## Two things, and the second is the better answer to the first

- **The divider is hard to hit.** Its handle is a 28-point circle over a picture that also accepts a drag, so a near-miss pans the photograph instead. The author says *"I always end up"* --- this is the common case, not an edge case.
- **Scroll should move the divider.** Which is not a workaround for the first: it makes the comparison usable at zoom, where the pan gesture is most wanted for the picture and the divider most needs to be moved without disturbing it.

The author has thought this through further than the report needs to: with scroll driving the divider, dragging remains free for panning, so zooming in to inspect stays possible either way.

## What already exists, and the trap in it

`CurtainGeometry.scrollBelongsToPicture(at:in:)` exists and is used --- **scroll is already bound to panning the picture**. Its own comment records why:

> *"`ComparisonView` panned the image from an `NSEvent` monitor that never asked where the pointer was, so scrolling the filter category strip moved the photograph."*

So this ticket cannot simply reassign scroll. There are two consumers of the same gesture over the same rectangle, and the design has to say which gets it and when. **That is a genuine design question and it belongs in guide 2.3 before any code**, not in a modifier.

## Violated criteria

- **AC90.14**: *"The curtain divider sits where the pointer is."* Unchanged as a rule --- this is about the divider being *reachable*, not about where it lands.
- Nothing covers the divider's hit area or which gesture drives it. Expect a backfill onto the #90 family.
- **UT-90.1** passed on re-offer and its geometry must not move: RT-96.13 asserts `dividerFraction` and `dividerX` as inverses and stays green.

## Debugging and fix procedure

1. **Measure the handle's hit area against its drawn size** before changing either. #66 established that `stroke` versus `strokeBorder` moves a 28-point handle to 29.5; the hit area may already differ from what the geometry assumes, and a fix aimed at the wrong one will look right and change nothing.
2. **Decide the gesture split in the design.** Scroll drives the divider, drag pans the picture, is the author's proposal and is coherent --- but it reverses an existing binding and guide 2.3 says nothing about either. Amend it first.
3. **Enlarging the hit area is separable from the scroll change** and is worth doing regardless: a hit area larger than the drawn handle is ordinary practice and costs nothing visually.
4. Keep the divider's arithmetic in `CurtainGeometry`. #90 moved it there after UT-90.1 failed on pointer alignment, and a hit-area change that reasons about coordinates outside it will drift.

## Test plan

- RT-136.1: the divider's hit area is at least as large as its drawn handle
- RT-136.2: a press within the hit area but outside the drawn handle moves the divider rather than panning the picture
- RT-136.3: scrolling with the pointer over the picture moves the divider
- RT-136.4: the divider's position after a scroll matches the same mapping a drag would produce
- RT-136.5: panning the picture is still possible by dragging
- RT-136.6: scrolling outside the picture still does not move anything
- UT-136.1: zoom in, move the divider by scrolling, and judge that inspecting a detail is workable

existed.*



## Acceptance criteria

- **AC136.1** The divider's hit area is larger than its drawn handle, and a press inside the hit area but outside the drawn circle moves the divider rather than panning the picture.
- **AC136.2** While the comparison curtain is up, scrolling with the pointer over the picture moves the divider, responding to whichever axis the scroll is dominantly along --- so a trackpad's sideways swipe and a wheel mouse, which reports only its vertical axis, both move it.
- **AC136.3** The position the divider reaches under a scroll is the same position the existing pointer mapping gives, so scrolling and dragging cannot disagree about where the divider belongs.
- **AC136.4** The divider stays within the bounds a drag observes, however far a scroll continues.
- **AC136.5** Dragging is how the picture is panned while the curtain is up, and it is the only way, so inspecting a detail at zoom remains possible without a second gesture competing for the divider's.
- **AC136.6** Scrolling with the pointer outside the picture moves neither the divider nor the picture.
- **AC136.7** The divider follows a scroll in the direction the system reports it, so a user whose natural-scrolling preference inverts their deltas gets the direction that preference implies rather than its opposite.
- **AC136.8** A single scroll moves the divider or the picture, never both, including when the pointer is over the divider's own handle.
