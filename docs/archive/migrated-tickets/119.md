## Original issue body

Child of delivery master #114 (post-MVP verification and defect closure).

## Problem statement

**Author, 2026-08-27:** *"you moved the progress spinner to the top of the image. It should be over the image, centered vertically and horizontally."*

## This is a change of intent, not a regression, and the distinction changes the work

The top placement is not an accident and was not introduced by recent work. It is specified:

- **AC90.13** in [docs/ACs.md](../blob/master/docs/ACs.md): *"...The indicator **sits at the top of the canvas** and may carry a background within its own bounds."*
- **RT-90.49**, currently ✅ passing: *"the indicator's vertical midpoint lies in the upper third of the canvas."*

Checked against the recent commits touching `MainView.swift` (`8bcba59`, `715aca1`, `93f04e0`): none altered the indicator's placement. The layout at `MainView.swift:144-149` is #90's, with its reasoning in a comment above it about the overlay having once been full-size.

Recording this because the fix requires **superseding a signed-off criterion and retiring a passing test**, which is a heavier action than fixing a defect and must be done deliberately rather than discovered halfway through.

## Criterion affected

**AC90.13's placement clause is superseded**, and a replacement criterion is needed. The rest of AC90.13 stands unchanged and is the part that matters most: the picture is drawn **unaltered** outside the indicator's own bounds, with no blur, dimming or material across it. That clause was written because a previous implementation filled the canvas with `.thinMaterial` and was reported as satisfied when it was not.

**Centring the indicator puts it directly over the picture**, which makes that clause harder to hold rather than easier. A centred badge must still carry its background within its own bounds and must not soften anything beneath it.

## Acceptance criteria

| ID | AC | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| AC119.1 | While an operation is under way, the progress indicator is centred over the picture, horizontally and vertically, and the picture is drawn unaltered outside the indicator's own bounds. | ⏳ RT-119.1: the indicator's horizontal midpoint agrees with the canvas's within two points<br>⏳ RT-119.2: the indicator's vertical midpoint agrees with the canvas's within two points<br>⏳ RT-119.3: with the indicator centred and the info panel shown, both are present and neither overlaps the other<br>⏳ RT-119.4: with the comparison showing, the indicator is centred over the curtain<br>⏳ UT-119.1: with an operation running, the indicator's position is judged |

**Key:** ✅ passing · ⏳ pending · ❌ failing · ~~🚫 removed~~

**AC90.13's area and unaltered-picture clauses are unchanged, and their tests stay where they are.** RT-90.44 (the indicator's area is under a quarter of the canvas) and RT-90.52 (the canvas outside its frame is identical before and during work) belong to AC90.13, which is superseded only in its *placement* clause. Restating them here would assert the same two properties twice across a criterion split in two, which is the duplication `ISSUES.md` warns about. They are cited rather than copied.

That said, centring does ask more of RT-90.52 than it did before: the badge now sits over the middle of the picture rather than over its edge. The test is unchanged; what it is holding has got harder, which is worth knowing when it is next read.

**RT-119.4 covers the comparison state**, which AC90.13 never had to answer because the indicator sat clear of the picture. The operation most likely to be running while a user is comparing is an upscale, which is exactly when they are watching.

**UT-119.1 is a user test** because whether a centred indicator reads as part of the work or as an obstruction is a judgement, and the author's original report is itself a judgement of the same kind. It is worded without a preferred answer: the author is shown a running operation and asked whether the indicator's position works. Centring a badge over a photograph is exactly the kind of change that can look worse in practice than it sounds in a ticket, and a leading prompt would hide that.

**Two points is the tolerance** for RT-119.1 and RT-119.2. The criterion means centred, not identical to the last decimal, and comparing laid-out `CGFloat` midpoints exactly would fail on rounding and retina scaling rather than on placement. Two points is well inside what anyone could perceive as off-centre and well outside layout noise.

**RT-119.5 holds what #90 already fixed.** `MainView.swift:127-145` stacks the indicator above the info panel in one `VStack`, and the comment there records why: as separate top-anchored children the panel drew over the indicator and hid it. Centring takes the indicator out of that stack, so the fix must not move the panel or reintroduce the overlap.

## Superseded

- **AC90.13's placement clause**, marked superseded by AC119.1 in `docs/ACs.md`, with the criterion text preserved and struck rather than deleted.
- **RT-90.49** (the indicator's vertical midpoint lies in the upper third of the canvas), marked `🚫 removed` with its identifier retired and not reused. It asserts the opposite of AC119.1 and cannot coexist with it.
- **UT-90.2** (the picture is unaltered while the ticker sits on top) is still pending on master #79 and will need re-offering against the new placement, since the author would be judging a different screen.

## Fix procedure

1. Read `MainView.swift:119-149`. The indicator is in a `VStack` anchored `.top` in the canvas `ZStack`, stacked above the info panel so the two do not overlap. Centring it means it no longer shares that stack, and the info panel's own placement must not move as a side effect.
2. Confirm the info panel and the indicator do not collide once separated. They were deliberately stacked in #90 because the panel drew over the indicator and hid it.
3. Verify the unaltered-picture guarantee still holds with the indicator over the picture's middle, which is RT-119.4's job.
