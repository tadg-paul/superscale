## Original issue body

Child of delivery master #120 (bring the build onto the design of record).

## Problem statement

**Observed by the author, user-test round of 2026-08-27:**

> *"the 'Applying filter' spinner is centered vertically now which is good. However it is too wide, and should be translucent to approx 70%. You could make it bigger, but the surrounding box should not be 4x wider than the text like it currently is. it just looks like we are obstructing a part of the photo without reason. You could show 'Applying Filter' on 2 lines with a spinner below in a box, make it slightly larger, but the tiny text and ultra-wide box is improper."*

## The centring is right; the box is not

**UT-119.1** (the centred progress indicator) failed on this. The vertical and horizontal centring #119 delivered is confirmed good and must not be disturbed.

The cause is visible in the source. `ProgressOverlay` carries `.frame(maxWidth: 420)` with the background applied **after** it, so the capsule takes the full 420 points whenever the canvas proposes at least that much, regardless of how short the message is. The type's own documentation comment says *"Sized to its own content, never to the canvas"* --- which is what it was meant to do and is not what that modifier does.

This also explains a detail from #119 that was left as a curiosity: `workingIndicator` matched several elements and the measured spinner sat 68 points left of the badge's centre. The spinner is at the left edge of a box four times wider than its content.

## Violated AC

- **AC119.1** in [docs/ACs.md](../blob/master/docs/ACs.md): the indicator is centred over the picture and the picture is drawn unaltered outside the indicator's own bounds. A background four times wider than its content obscures picture for no reason, which is the spirit of the clause even if the letter is arguably satisfied.
- **AC90.13**, whose surviving clauses require the picture to be drawn unaltered outside the indicator's bounds and the indicator's area to stay under a quarter of the canvas. RT-90.44 asserts the area bound and must stay green.

`audit-acs` must rule on whether translucency is inside AC90.13's *"may carry a background within its own bounds"* or is a new condition needing a backfill. It is a change to what the background may be, not only to how wide it is.

## The author's specification

- The background is sized to its content, not to a fixed maximum.
- Approximately 70% opacity.
- The indicator may be **larger** overall: the objection is to tiny text in a wide box, not to the indicator's presence.
- "Applying Filter" may be set over two lines with the spinner beneath.

Layout beyond that is not specified and the audits must not invent detail the author did not give. Where a choice is genuinely open, record it as a decision on this ticket.

## Debugging and fix procedure

1. **Measure before changing.** Record the rendered background width against the text width, for a short message and a long one. The 4x figure is the author's observation of one case and the fix must hold for both.
2. **The `.frame(maxWidth:)` is the hypothesis, not the finding.** A `maxWidth` frame takes the proposal clamped to the maximum rather than shrinking to its child, and the background is applied outside it. Confirm by measurement before editing.
3. **A maximum width is still wanted**, so a long progress message does not run the full width of a wide canvas. The fix is to size to content *up to* a maximum, not to remove the bound.
4. **Do not disturb the centring.** RT-119.1 and RT-119.2 must stay green, and RT-90.44's area bound must hold at the larger size the author permits.
5. `ProgressOverlay` publishes one combined accessibility element as of #119. Any re-layout must keep that; without it the measurement tests read the spinner instead of the badge.

## Test plan

- RT-128.1: the indicator's background width tracks its content rather than a fixed maximum
- RT-128.2: a long message is bounded rather than running the full canvas width
- RT-128.3: the indicator remains horizontally and vertically centred over the picture
- RT-128.4: the indicator's area stays under a quarter of the canvas at its larger size
- RT-128.5: the indicator remains one addressable element carrying its message
- UT-119.1 re-offered: start an operation and judge the indicator's size, proportions and translucency
