## What this issue is, and is not

## What can be established without guessing

Three things are measurable or readable now:

1. **Where the time actually goes.** An apply is not one operation: it is a possible raise to the filterable minimum (a real Neural Engine run of several seconds), an upload of the base to provider storage, the generation request itself, then a download and a local upscale of the result. **The author perceives one wait.** Attributing it to grok before measuring the parts would be the same error this delivery has made three times.
2. **What the request carries.** `docs/FAL_REQUEST_REFERENCE.md` and `FalGenerationRequest` record what is sent. Image size reaching the provider is the parameter most likely to move latency, and the minimum-resolution floor and the area ceiling both act on it.
3. **What pix does differently.** The author refers to another project. Its request shape, model and image sizing are the comparison, and reading it is the point of the reference.

## Constraints that bound any recommendation

- **OT-107.1 to OT-107.4 are not repeated.** Live provider calls cost real money and the author has said so. Any timing evidence from a live call must come from what has already been recorded, or be explicitly authorized.
- Guide section 6 keeps pricing and account surfaces paused; nothing here reopens them.
- The MVP ships one model, `xai/grok-imagine-image`. Changing it is out of scope for this issue and would be the author's decision in any case.

## Procedure

## Finding: why a filter takes as long as it does, and the two things worth changing

**Investigation only. No code changed, and no provider call was made** --- OT-107.x costs money and
was not re-run. Everything below is read from the code, with file and line references so you can
check me.

### First, the honest limit on this answer

**I cannot compare us against pix.** I have not seen it, cannot time it, and have no visibility into
what it does between your click and your picture. Anything I said about *why it is faster* would be
invention dressed as analysis.

What I can do is account for the time **our** side spends, and say which of it is ours to remove.
That is what follows.

### What one Apply actually costs

Four network round trips, in strict sequence, plus local work before them:

| Step | Where | What crosses the wire |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Raise to the filterable minimum, if the picture is under 1024px | local Core ML | nothing |
| 1. `storage/upload/initiate` | `FalStorageClient.swift:145` | a small JSON POST |
| 2. `PUT` the bytes | `FalStorageClient.swift:128` | **the whole image, every time** |
| 3. `generate` | `FalGenerationClient.swift:73` | small JSON, blocks until the model finishes |
| 4. `GET` the result | `FalGenerationClient.swift:101` | the returned image |

Step 3 is the provider's own model time and is not ours. **Steps 1 and 2 are ours, and they are
the two I would change.**

### Finding 1 --- we re-upload the same picture for every filter

`submitFilter` calls `uploadReference` unconditionally on every apply
(`MainView.swift:534`). The coordinator is a straight pass-through
(`GenerationCoordinator.swift:192-197`), and `FalStorageClient.upload` reads the file and pushes the
full bytes each time (`FalStorageClient.swift:111-130`).

So trying six filters on one photograph uploads that photograph **six times**, byte for byte
identical. Two of the four round trips, including the only large one, are pure repetition from the
second filter onward.

**This is the one I would do.** The asset graph already holds what the cache needs to be keyed on ---
`#124` established that the graph answers "have we done this before" without a second store --- and
the same reasoning applies to "have we uploaded this before". A reference URL held against the
asset's identity, invalidated when the asset changes, would remove steps 1 and 2 for every filter
after the first on a given picture.

**The caveat I cannot resolve from here:** FAL's storage URLs may expire, and I do not know their
lifetime. That is a question for their documentation before anyone builds this, and it decides
whether the cache needs a age limit or a retry-on-404. **It does not change the recommendation, only
its shape.**

### Finding 2 --- we upload full-resolution pictures to a model that works near one megapixel

`MinimumResolution.longEdge` is 1024 and is a **floor with no ceiling**
(`MinimumResolution.swift:56`). Nothing between the base asset and the wire reduces anything: the
upload is handed `asset.fileURL` and sends that file as it is.

A 6000px photograph is roughly **34 times** the pixel count of a 1024px one, and PNG size tracks
pixel count fairly closely. So on a large original, step 2 is not slightly slower --- it is a
different order of magnitude, and it is upload bandwidth, which on most domestic connections is the
slowest direction.

Grok resizes what it receives anyway. Guide 2.5's own note records it squares anything whose short
edge is under 1024, and `editAcceptsSizing: false` in `FalRequestBuilder.swift` records that its edit
endpoint will not even accept a sizing parameter --- so the pixels above what the model uses are
being paid for in time and discarded at the other end.

**Why I am recommending this more cautiously than the first.** Sending a reduced copy means the
filter's result derives from a reduction of your picture rather than from your picture. For this
application that may well be correct --- the result comes back at model resolution regardless, and
the upscaler is the thing that puts the pixels back --- but it changes what the provider sees, and
**that is your call and not mine.**

### What I would not do

- **Not parallelism.** The four steps are strictly dependent: you cannot generate before the upload
  finishes and cannot download before the model does. There is nothing here to overlap.
- **Not the polling.** We use the blocking endpoint, not the queue, so no poll interval is adding
  latency. This is already the faster of FAL's two shapes.
- **Not the local raise.** It only runs for pictures under 1024px, it is Neural Engine work rather
  than network, and removing it would send the provider something below the size it wants.

### Recommended order

1. **Cache the uploaded reference per asset.** Clear win, no change to what the provider sees, and
   the graph already knows the identity to key it on. Needs the URL-lifetime question answered first.
2. **Decide whether to cap what is sent.** Potentially the larger saving of the two on your own
   pictures, but it changes the input to a paid model, so it wants your decision before any code.

Neither is in this round's scope and I have not started either. **Say which you want and I will
raise it as its own child** --- and if the answer is that the current speed is acceptable now that
you can see what it is made of, that is a legitimate outcome and this ticket closes on it.
