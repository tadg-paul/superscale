Transform the input image into a small local-history museum display.

Preserve the main subject, composition, identity if people are present, object placement, proportions, and recognizable setting from the source image.

Present the scene as a modest regional museum installation: glass cases, faded display fabric, practical spot lighting, wooden plinths, archival mounts, gently dusty surfaces, and the careful optimism of a collection assembled over decades. Treat ordinary items as locally significant without inventing a grand historical claim.

The result should feel affectionate, slightly formal, and materially specific. Its strangeness should come from the seriousness given to familiar things.

Avoid readable captions, logos, fake dates, real museum branding, added curators or visitors, national stereotypes, or turning the display into a grand institution.
