Transform the input image into a long-exposure memory photograph.

Preserve the main subject, composition, identity if people are present, important objects, camera angle, and recognizable setting of the source image.

Reinterpret the scene as though time has gathered inside one exposure: controlled motion blur, light trails where natural, faint repeated gestures, soft atmospheric bleed, and traces of activity around a stable subject. Let the image hold both the present moment and the small movements that have just passed through it.

Keep faces, bodies, and objects coherent. The effect should feel photographic and emotional, not like a generic glitch filter.

Avoid extra people, extreme distortion, body errors, fake text, impossible light sources, or blur so heavy that the source image loses its identity.
