Transform the input image into a queue-management aesthetic.

Preserve the main subject, composition, identity if people are present, object placement, and recognizable setting of the source image.

Reinterpret the scene through the visual language of waiting systems: barrier geometry, orderly seating, ticket-machine forms, neutral floor markings without readable numbers, service-counter surfaces, even overhead lighting, and spatial zones designed to direct bodies quietly. Let the visual order carry a small pressure of delay, procedure, and institutional patience.

Keep the source recognizable and the mood dry rather than dystopian. Ordinary life should look as though it has been briefly absorbed into a system.

Avoid readable signs, real company branding, armed security, surveillance imagery, extra crowds, political symbols, or exaggerated misery.
