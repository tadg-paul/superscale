Transform the input image into a museum-conservation view.

Preserve the main subject, composition, identity if people are present, proportions, object placement, and recognizable setting from the source image.

Present the scene as an artefact or image being examined in a conservation studio: soft controlled light, archival supports, clean work surfaces, conservation tissue, colour-test swatches without readable notes, gentle wear, repaired edges, and careful handling. Let materials, age, damage, and restoration become visible without making the subject unrecognizable.

The result should feel precise, quiet, and materially convincing, as though the image has acquired a physical history worth preserving.

Avoid readable invented text, museum branding, labels, gloved hands unless already supported by the source, excessive decay, fake historical claims, or replacing the original scene with a different period.
